Leadership Portfolio

Selected work from my first year as Vice Principal at Colegio Panamericano: curriculum coordination, teacher development, and responsible AI policy. These examples show the systems I helped develop with colleagues and the practical tools behind the work.

01 / Curriculum leadership

Connecting learning across grades and subjects

Teachers need a shared view of what students have learned, what comes next, and how individual courses fit into a coherent pathway.

I coordinated vertical alignment work across Mathematics, Science, English, and Spanish, and helped bring departmental course descriptions into an Upper School catalog draft. The work connects standards, units, prerequisite skills, and advanced course pathways.

Work products: four subject alignment documents, departmental course descriptions, and a consolidated catalog draft. The catalog remains a development project pending final review.

Explore the planning framework

Adapted portfolio example

  1. Identify the priority standards and intended learning.
  2. Match each learning outcome to formative and summative evidence.
  3. Plan instruction, practice, feedback, and opportunities to revise.
  4. Specify prerequisite skills and readiness for the next grade or course.
  5. Review learning evidence and agree on departmental follow-up.

This sequence brings curriculum, assessment, and instruction into one conversation while preserving teachers’ flexibility in lesson design.

02 / Teacher development

Making feedback specific and actionable

Useful coaching gives teachers clear next steps and opportunities to see, practice, and reflect on effective instruction.

I developed observation guidance, evaluation timelines, and a peer-coaching framework that links visits to colleagues’ classrooms with implementation goals, reflection, and follow-up. My feedback recognizes instructional strengths and identifies concrete priorities such as lesson clarity, formative questioning, student participation, and classroom routines.

Work products: observation process presentation, annual evaluation guide, peer-observation and reflection forms, and written feedback.

Explore the coaching cycle

Adapted portfolio example

  1. Agree on a focused instructional goal and observable indicators.
  2. Observe a colleague and identify strategies to adapt.
  3. Try selected strategies in a planned lesson.
  4. Gather classroom evidence and reflect on what changed.
  5. Use a follow-up conversation to refine the next step.

For example, a focus on checking understanding can connect modeled explanations, purposeful questions, and adjustments before independent practice.

03 / Responsible AI leadership

Turning emerging technology into clear expectations

Schools need practical guidance on how AI can support learning while protecting academic integrity and professional judgment.

I helped lead a cross-department AI committee and developed bilingual policy language addressing student disclosure, teacher-authorized use, privacy, review of AI-generated materials, and responses to academic misuse. The work translated broad principles into expectations for daily school practice.

Work products: bilingual policy-change summary and supporting policy research. This case study focuses on policy development.

Explore the policy framework

Adapted portfolio example

  • Set clear expectations for permitted assistance and disclosure.
  • Require students to explain and demonstrate their own understanding.
  • Keep teachers responsible for reviewing and adapting materials.
  • Protect student and staff information.
  • Use a clear review process when concerns arise.

The focus is transparent use, meaningful learning, and human oversight.

Book, Research, and Writing

Early Dismissal

My published book, Early Dismissal: Addressing the Teacher Retention Crisis — Why It Matters and What Can Be Done, grew out of my PhD research in Educational Leadership and my experience across public, private, and international schools.

The book focuses on teacher retention, leadership trust, school culture, teacher support, and the practical conditions that influence whether educators stay or leave.

My research continues to shape how I think about onboarding, workload, teacher well-being, leadership credibility, and the day-to-day systems that help schools function better.
